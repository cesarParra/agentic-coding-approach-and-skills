Nothing else: not what the code does, why it exists, what it replaced, what a neighbour does, or what sits "below". A comment that could be a name becomes the name. Prose opens with what the reader needs to do or know, and every heading states its point. Prose carries facts and steps only: no backstory, no asides to the reader, no intensifiers, no hedging, no recap, no dashes as punctuation, and no foil set up to be knocked down. Use strong verbs, positive phrasing and short sentences. Load broom:writing before writing a guide, README or any document others will read. A judge with no context reads every edit, and its verdict is applied, never argued. A title is an index entry — a commit subject, a pull request or issue title, a task title — read beside thirty others with the body closed: lead with the verb, name the thing by its real identifier, say what happens to it. No metaphor, no problem stated as an observation, no clause that needs the body open. The same judge reads it at the door.
